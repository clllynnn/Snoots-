# Snoots! UX Audit — Core Modules

## Overview

This UX audit evaluates four core product modules of Snoots! through the lens of user needs, behavioral insights, and market validation. Each module is assessed based on clarity of user intent, problem-solution fit, and opportunity for differentiation in the Taiwan pet-tech landscape.

## Early-Stage Product Strategy

### Focused Entry Point Recommendation

For the initial version (MVP), Snoots! should prioritize:

- Playdate Matching
- Pet-Friendly Maps

### Rationale

- Both features solve immediate, high-friction real-world problems.
- They provide clear, utility-driven value rather than only engagement.
- They naturally encourage repeat usage and habit formation.
- They create strong foundations for trust and data accuracy.

### Strategic Benefits

- Faster user adoption through practical daily use cases.
- Easier differentiation from generic social platforms.
- Builds location-based network effects.
- Establishes Snoots! as a reliable tool, not just a social app.

### Expansion Path

Once trust and a user base are established:

1. Layer in Social Feed (content + identity).
2. Integrate Emergency Guidance (high-trust utility layer).

## Module 1: Social Feed

### User Intent

Dog owners want to share updates about their pets using photos and descriptive tags, while connecting with a like-minded community.

### UX Evaluation

- Strong emotional driver: “pet as family member.”
- Clear content creation loop: post → engage → validate identity.
- Tagging system supports discoverability and identity expression.

### Pain Points / Gaps

- Generic social feeds risk becoming noisy without meaningful filtering.
- Lack of trust signals may reduce perceived credibility of posts.

### Market Insights

- Pet ownership has evolved into “pet parenting,” increasing emotional investment.
- 82% of dog owners trust peer-generated content over traditional marketing.
- In Taiwan, dogs are culturally treated as family (“毛小孩”).
- Urban owners—young professionals and couples—show high anxiety around pet health.

### Opportunities

- Introduce trust indicators (verified owners, health tags).
- Smart feed ranking based on relevance and credibility.
- Highlight milestones and lifecycle moments.

## Module 2: Playdate Matching

### User Intent

Dog owners want to safely find compatible playmates for their pets based on specific traits.

### UX Evaluation

- Familiar interaction model: swipe-based matching.
- Clear value: safety and compatibility.
- High engagement potential through gamification.

### Pain Points / Gaps

- Safety concerns, including aggression and vaccination status.
- Lack of trust between unknown owners.
- Coordination friction for real-world meetups.

### Market Insights

- Strong demand has been validated by “dog dating” apps in major cities.
- In Taiwan, public dog parks exist, but owners report anxiety about uncontrolled environments, mixed dog sizes, and unverified health status.

### Opportunities

- Introduce a compatibility scoring system.
- Require profile verification for vaccination and behavior tags.
- Enable controlled meetup-planning tools.

## Module 3: Pet-Friendly Maps

### User Intent

Dog owners want accurate, detailed information about pet policies at local venues.

### UX Evaluation

- High utility in real-world scenarios.
- Location-based filtering aligns with user expectations.
- Reduces friction in lifestyle integration with pets.

### Pain Points / Gaps

- Existing platforms provide vague or misleading “pet-friendly” labels.
- Lack of standardized policy details.
- High consequence of misinformation: rejection at the venue.

### Market Insights

- Current solutions, such as generic map platforms, fail to verify policies.
- Taiwan-specific constraints include complex and conditional pet rules, such as dogs being allowed only in strollers, size-based restrictions, and mandatory accessories such as courtesy belts.

### Opportunities

- Provide granular policy filters for indoor access, size limits, and equipment requirements.
- Enable community verification and updates.
- Highlight certainty indicators for verified versus user-reported information.

## Module 4: Emergency Guidance

### User Intent

In urgent situations, dog owners need immediate access to nearby emergency veterinary services.

### UX Evaluation

- Critical, time-sensitive use case.
- Requires extreme clarity and speed.
- Minimal interaction steps.

### Pain Points / Gaps

- Difficulty identifying truly open 24/7 veterinary hospitals.
- Outdated or inaccurate operating hours.
- High stress amplifies usability issues.

### Market Insights

- Emergency veterinary services are scarce outside major city centers.
- Many clinics have reduced or eliminated overnight services because of staffing shortages.

### Opportunities

- One-tap emergency locator button.
- Real-time availability status.
- Prioritize distance and capability, including trauma and surgery.
- Provide navigation and contact instantly.

## Key Cross-Module Insights

### 1. Trust Is the Core UX Currency

Across all modules, users require verified information, reliable peer input, and safety assurances.

### 2. Taiwan-Specific Localization Is Critical

Snoots! must account for dense urban living constraints, complex pet policies, and the cultural framing of pets as children.

### 3. Anxiety Reduction Is a Primary Value Driver

Users consistently experience stress around health decisions, social safety, and public acceptance.

### 4. Opportunity for Product Differentiation

Snoots! can win by structuring fragmented information, verifying real-world data, and designing for emotional reassurance—not just functionality.

## Conclusion

Snoots! addresses high-demand, emotionally driven user needs in an underserved and fragmented market. By focusing early on Playdate Matching and Pet-Friendly Maps, the product can establish immediate real-world value, build trust, and create a strong foundation for expanding into social and high-trust utility features.
